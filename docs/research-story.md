# Research story

This repository documents a staged investigation of circular-dichroism (CD)
and magneto-circular-dichroism (MCD) in chiral assemblies of cobalt ferrite
(CFO) particles. It is a record of models, checks, negative results, and open
tests. It is not a claim that one fitted curve has already identified the
microscopic origin of the experimental signal.

## The question

The starting point was a familiar model from chiral plasmonic assemblies:
represent each particle by an electric dipole and let a Green tensor propagate
the field between particles. A helical arrangement can then distinguish left
and right circular polarization. The same reduction is useful for CFO, but it
leaves open several physically different possibilities:

* the material response may be anisotropic and magnetization dependent;
* a finite particle may require magnetic-dipole or higher-multipole terms;
* electric dipoles in a helix may form a collective circulating response even
  when each particle has no intrinsic magnetic dipole;
* substrate, film orientation, disorder, and the measured observable may differ
  from the ideal free-space calculation.

The project therefore uses the following causal chain rather than treating an
experimental `g` value as a model parameter:

```text
material epsilon(lambda, M)
  -> finite-particle response (p, m, Q, alpha, or T-matrix)
  -> inter-particle propagation and multiple scattering
  -> C_ext, C_abs, C_sca for LCP/RCP
  -> CD, MCD, and experimental g_CD
```

Here `epsilon` is a material constitutive response, `alpha` is a reduced
finite-particle response, and `g_CD` is an observable at the end of the chain.
They are not interchangeable fitting knobs.

## How the interpretation changed

### 1. Establish a transparent electric-dipole baseline

The first step was a small Python 3N coupled-dipole implementation with
separate material, geometry, polarizability, Green-tensor, incident-field, and
observable modules. An existing gold-helix calculation served as a sign and
scale benchmark. This established a reproducible electric-dipole baseline
without assuming a magnetic or magnetoelectric coupling.

The CFO baseline reproduced the expected handedness reversal between mirror
geometries. It did not reproduce the experimental magnitude. More elaborate
bead, rod, tilted-array, and fixed-incidence variants changed the spectral
shape but did not remove the scale gap. Fixed incidence was therefore retained
as an experimental-condition check, not treated as a complete thin-film model.

### 2. Separate the different meanings of “magnetic”

The mismatch could not be assigned to one unspecified magnetic term. Three
effects must be kept separate:

1. ordinary single-particle Mie channels such as electric dipole, magnetic
   dipole, and electric quadrupole;
2. a magnetization-dependent off-diagonal material response, represented by
   `epsilon_xy`, which first changes the electric response tensor;
3. a collective magnetic-like moment generated by phase-shifted electric
   dipoles arranged in a helix.

The tensor CDM calculation shows that a supplied `+M/-M` material tensor can
produce a sizable magnetization-odd response in the reduced model. That result
motivates an MCD study, but it is not a proof that the same term explains the
R/S structural CD. Likewise, the helix can have a collective effective moment,
but an origin-dependent multipole proxy is not an intrinsic single-particle
`alpha_HE` measurement.

The inverse coupling probes had a narrower role: they estimated the size of an
effective channel needed to reach an experimental-scale signal. They did not
derive a CFO magnetoelectric constant. In particular, an empirical `eta` or
`beta` must not be relabeled as a measured `alpha_EH` or `alpha_HE`.

### 3. Use a single-particle benchmark before increasing model order

Earlier project work compared a scalar CFO sphere with the exact
homogeneous-sphere Mie solution over the tested size range. That historical run
reported an electric-dipole-dominated scalar response and agreement between
the electric-dipole polarizability and the Mie electric-dipole channel. The
CFO input and its detailed output are not included in this public seed package,
so readers cannot reproduce that material-specific result here.

The public Mie script uses a generated synthetic dielectric table. Its passing
checks test the coefficient sums, energy identity, multipole convergence, and
the code's electric-dipole implementation for that synthetic sphere. They do
not confirm the historical CFO result or test `epsilon_xy`, a magnetic tensor,
a substrate, or a magnetized anisotropic bead.

The historical CFO result narrowed the original interpretation: it did not
support replacing every scalar bead with a full multipole model by default. A
full-wave solver still needs separate tests for tensor material response,
substrates, and the finite-particle response of magnetized anisotropic beads.

The planned full-wave role follows from this distinction. A Maxwell solver
should first reproduce the scalar benchmark, then test a tensor single
particle, a dimer, and a small helix. Only after those checks should a reduced
response be used for a large assembly.

### 4. Reframe CD inversion as a conditional inverse problem

The September 2026 review of the data chain separated the CD/absorbance/
`g` tables used as experimental targets from the UV–vis inputs used in an
early scalar-material reconstruction. It also documented baseline handling and
the finite spectral window. This matters because an endpoint with zero modeled
loss is not an independent measurement that the material is lossless.

The direct-CD inversion was then treated as a conditional question: under a
fixed geometry, background, normalization, and particle-response model, what
effective scalar response can reproduce the CD target? A single CD value does
not independently determine both real and imaginary parts of a complex
dielectric function, and a common mirror model cannot force two experimental
branches to have exactly the same overall scale. A separately fitted curve can
therefore reproduce a training spectrum while absorbing missing physics and
still fail to establish a unique material response.

An indexing error in an earlier material interpolation path was found and
corrected before the later publication-oriented decomposition was treated as a
valid result. This is a key reproducibility rule for this repository: a result
is tied to the exact input ordering, interpolation convention, normalization,
and validation record, not merely to a figure that looks plausible.

The September 2026 work also separated several questions that had previously
been mixed together: recovering an experimental target table, reconstructing a
scalar material input from optical data, and asking what response is required
to reproduce CD alone. The final CD-only inverse uses the full target band and
keeps the common mirror relation explicit. It can fit a training target under
fixed conventions, but its residual, scale convention, and non-uniqueness are
part of the result. It does not use that fit to claim a unique material
function, to replace an independent absorption measurement, or to infer a
magnetoelectric tensor.

### 5. Keep the PS-b-PAA spectral branch separate

The later organic-material work answers a different question. It constructs a
literature-constrained effective PS-b-PAA optical input from constituent `n,k`
data using the specified equal-weight complex-index mixing rule, then compares
two uniform-material reference spectra on the same helix geometry. A smooth,
non-negative, regularized decomposition of an experimental CD target provides
an energy-region attribution under those assumptions.

This branch is phenomenological. It does not model an explicit CFO core,
polymer shell, block segregation, chain conformation, substrate, or detector
collection angle. The fitted coefficients are not mass fractions, volume
fractions, surface coverage, or independent PS/PAA concentrations. The
finite-band Kramers–Kronig checks are diagnostics of selected source curves;
they are not a complete causality proof for the mixed effective material.

The branch must remain visibly separate from the CFO magnetic-mechanism line.
It can document the code and public-source material construction if those
inputs are redistributable. Partner-provided experimental tables, unpublished
spectra, collaboration drafts, and any derived output that exposes restricted
values stay outside this public repository until explicit redistribution rights
are recorded.

### 6. Clarify theory and experimental scale factors

The mirror-geometry relation gives a theoretical S branch with the opposite
sign and the same model normalization as the R branch. An empirical overall
factor used to compare two experimental branches accounts for sample or
measurement scale; it is not a material constant, a polymer fraction, or a
chirality parameter. Public figures must label these two operations separately
so that a plotting rescale is not mistaken for a physical prediction.

The late-September packaging work reorganized already calculated material,
coefficient, and geometry views: publication panels were exported as named
plotting tables, and the helix snapshot was rendered directly from the model
coordinates. These operations do not add physical evidence. The geometry image
shows the idealized CDM coordinates, not a reconstruction of the sample's
morphology or a map of alternating CFO and polymer particles.

## Current state of the research

### Established within the stated models

* The public Python electric-dipole example runs with a synthetic input and
  passes its single-particle, mirror-sign, and matrix-shape checks.
* Earlier project work compared a scalar CFO sphere with an independent Mie
  solution, including channel, passivity, and energy-consistency checks. Its
  CFO input and detailed outputs are not included in this public package.
* Earlier pure-electric CFO helix calculations produced structural handedness
  reversal below the experimental target magnitude. The current public demo
  does not reproduce that material-specific comparison.
* The material, finite-particle, propagation, and observable layers are now
  recorded as distinct objects.

### Diagnostic, not a mechanism proof

* Tensor CDM with `+M/-M` input estimates the possible scale of a
  magnetization-odd signal.
* Collective-moment and effective-bianisotropy calculations show what a pure
  electric helix can look like after coarse graining.
* Coupling inversions estimate a missing-response scale.
* CD-only dielectric inversions and PS-b-PAA spectral decompositions select
  conditional mathematical representatives under fixed constraints.

### Still required before a microscopic claim

* scalar and tensor single-particle full-wave benchmarks;
* dimer and small-helix comparisons between full wave and reduced models;
* a complete R/S × `+M/-M` parity decomposition;
* mesh, exterior-domain, passivity, and energy-conservation checks for the
  full-wave route;
* explicit tests of substrate, particle-size/shape distributions, magnetic
  domains, and measurement collection conditions where needed.

The public repository contains a validated synthetic code baseline and records
historical CFO calculations separately from the included, rerunnable evidence.
The microscopic origin of the experimental CD/MCD mismatch remains open.
